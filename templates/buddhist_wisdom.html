MINDFULNESS · COMPASSION · WISDOM

Buddhist Wisdom.

Explore foundational teachings as invitations to learn, reflect and practice with care. Traditions and interpretations vary across Buddhist schools.

FOUNDATIONAL TEACHING

The Four Noble Truths

  1. Dukkha: Life includes unsatisfactoriness, stress and change.
  2. Origin: Craving and clinging contribute to suffering.
  3. Cessation: Freedom from craving is described as the cessation of suffering.
  4. Path: The Noble Eightfold Path is taught as a way of practice.

Source tradition: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11). This is a concise paraphrase, not a direct quotation.

PRACTICE

The Noble Eightfold Path

Right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration.

Rather than treating these as a checklist for perfection, use them as connected areas for learning and daily practice.

Source tradition: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11).

DAILY REFLECTION

Mindfulness and the present moment

Pause and notice what is happening in the body and mind. Recognize thoughts and feelings without immediately treating them as commands.

Try a gentle practice: take three natural breaths, notice the body, and return attention without judging yourself for distraction.

Related source: Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10). This practice summary is educational, not a substitute for instruction from a qualified teacher.

COMPASSION

Goodwill toward yourself and others

Consider wishing safety, ease and wellbeing for yourself and for others. Compassion does not require ignoring harm or abandoning healthy boundaries.

Related source tradition: Karaṇīya Mettā Sutta (Snp 1.8). Wording and practice vary among traditions.
